      How a crystal habit is built

      Every crystal on this site comes from the same six steps: lattice, face normals, symmetry, half-space cuts, relative development and normalization. Pick any crystal and walk through the steps. In step 5 you can drag the sliders and watch the habit change.

      Step 1

          The general recipe

          1. Lattice to face normals. nhkl = h a* + k b* + l c*, so the cell geometry fixes every interfacial angle. Hexagonal minerals use Miller–Bravais (hkil) indices, with i dropped.
          2. Point-group symmetry. Each form {hkl} expands to all of its equivalent faces, which share one central distance.
          3. Half-space intersection. The crystal is the convex polyhedron n·x ≤ d over all faces, the Gibbs–Wulff construction. A plane that misses the solid leaves no face.
          4. Relative development. A typology ratio such as Pupin's {100}:{110} becomes a perimeter fraction (prisms) or an area fraction (terminations), and the central distances are solved by bisection.
          5. Elongation. Translating the two terminations rigidly along the axis lengthens the prism without changing the terminations: d′ = d + |n·u|·t.
          6. Normalization and export. The crystal is scaled to unit volume and written as a binary STL and an exact planar B-rep STEP file.

          The shapes are idealized: convex, unabraded, untwinned and unzoned. The browser rebuilds each crystal from the same half-spaces as the Python generator and reproduces every published geometry (face counts identical, volumes within 10⁻⁷).
